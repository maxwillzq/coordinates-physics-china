### Claim 1: The *Mozi* (*墨经*) contains significant optical and mechanical knowledge.
- **Draft Location**: `part1.md`, line 7.
- **Verification Analysis**:
    - **Party 1 (Recollections/Primary Text)**: The *Mozi* text itself, specifically the "Mohist Canon" (墨经), contains descriptions of pinhole imaging, reflection in plane and curved mirrors, and discussions on force and motion.
    - **Party 2 (Archives)**: The survival of the *Mozi* text in the Daoist Canon (道藏) and subsequent traditional compilations serves as the archival record of these statements.
    - **Party 3 (International Evaluation)**: Joseph Needham's *Science and Civilisation in China*, Volume 4, Part 1 (Physics) extensively analyzes these passages and confirms that Mohist optics was advanced for its time, comparable to or exceeding contemporary Greek developments in some aspects.
- **Status**: **Verified** by academic consensus.

### Claim 2: Copernicus' heliocentric theory marked the beginning of the scientific revolution by challenging the Aristotelian system.
- **Draft Location**: `part1.md`, line 4.
- **Verification Analysis**:
    - **Party 1 (Recollections)**: Copernicus' own work, *De revolutionibus orbium coelestium* (1543), where he details his model and his reasoning.
    - **Party 2 (Archives)**: The records of the Catholic Church (e.g., the decision to place the book on the *Index Librorum Prohibitorum* in 1616) and the correspondence of contemporary astronomers like Galileo and Kepler who engaged with the text.
    - **Party 3 (International Evaluation)**: Thomas Kuhn's *The Copernican Revolution* (1957) provides a standard independent academic evaluation of how this event catalyzed the shift from Aristotelian to modern physics.
- **Status**: **Verified** by academic consensus.

### Claim 3: The Translation House of the Jiangnan Arsenal (江南制造局翻译馆) systematically translated western physics works during the Self-Strengthening Movement.
- **Draft Location**: `part2.md`, line 7.
- **Verification Analysis**:
    - **Party 1 (Recollections)**: Accounts by translators such as Xu Shou (徐寿) and John Fryer (傅兰雅), who recorded their methods and the difficulties they faced in translating scientific terms.
    - **Party 2 (Archives)**: The published translation works themselves (e.g., *Li Xue* 理想 - Mechanics, *Ge Zhi Qi Meng* 格致启蒙) and official Qing dynasty documents regarding the establishment and funding of the Arsenal.
    - **Party 3 (International Evaluation)**: Benjamin Elman's *On Their Own Terms: Science in China, 1550-1900* (2005) provides a comprehensive study of the impact and scope of these translations, confirming their role in systematically introducing physics to China.
- **Status**: **Verified** by academic consensus.
