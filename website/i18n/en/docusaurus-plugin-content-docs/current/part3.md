# Part 3: The Quantum Revolution and the Pioneers of "Flesh-and-Blood Interfacing" (1900–1949)

> *"A university is not defined by large buildings, but by big masters."* —— Mei Yiqi

![Southwest Joint University and Quantum Orbitals Illustration](pathname:///img/part3_illustration.png)

## World Axis
The first half of the 20th century was the most exciting era in the history of physics. Starting from 1900 when Planck proposed the energy quantum hypothesis, the clear sky of classical physics dissolved, triggering a disruptive "Quantum Revolution." Subsequently, Einstein proposed the theory of relativity, completely reshaping the view of space and time; and a large number of physicists such as Bohr, Heisenberg, Schrödinger, and Dirac constructed the magnificent theoretical edifice of quantum mechanics in just two or three decades. Physics moved from qualitative to quantitative probability, and from continuity to discreteness, and the paradigm underwent a fundamental shift. The "World Axis" of this period was the wild advancement of quantum theory and relativity, which cleared the theoretical obstacles for humanity to enter the nuclear energy and information age.

## China Point
While world physics was undergoing a violent paradigm shift, China was experiencing the most severe social turbulence and war baptism in modern history. However, it was exactly this batch of pioneers called "flesh-and-blood interfacing" who, under the extreme predicament of a poor and weak country and continuous wars, arduously initiated the institutionalization and academic transplantation process of modern physics in China. The first batch of Gengzi indemnity students represented by Hu Gangfu, Rao Yutai, Ye Qisun, and Wu Youxun (the "Four Great Mentors" of Chinese physics), resolutely returned to China after completing their studies, establishing physics departments and research institutes in universities like Southeast, Tsinghua, Peking, and Zhejiang University. Wu Youxun precisely verified the Compton effect, Zhao Zhongyao first observed positron annihilation and pair production, and the teachers and students of Southwest Associated University maintained world-class teaching and research levels in thatched huts while dodging bombings. They trained future Nobel laureates in physics, Tsung-Dao Lee and Yang Chen-Ning, as well as the founding fathers of the "Two Bombs, One Satellite" project, writing a miracle in the history of science and education.

## Coordinate Value
From the perspective of the coordinate system of "World Axis - China Point," Chinese physics in this period was in the stage of "catching up and laying foundations." Faced with the wildly advancing quantum revolution in the West, the strategy of Chinese physicists was "flesh-and-blood interfacing"—using their blood and flesh and the responsibility of intellectuals to build the discipline edifice of modern physics on ruins. From the perspective of the philosophy of science, this was not only a discipline catch-up but also a flesh-and-blood answer to the "Needham Question." While Western physicists were debating the essence of the microscopic world in the storm of quantum paradigm shift, the first generation of Chinese physicists faced the "double predicament of catch-up players": they had to make up for the lessons of classical physics and simultaneously keep up with the latest frontier of quantum mechanics, while also establishing the education and research institutions of modern science in the war. Although there was still a gap in the absolute height of pure theoretical innovation compared with the world's top level, Chinese physics completed the discipline institutionalization from scratch in such a short time and trained scientific giants who would break parity conservation and enter the international frontier in the future. Its coordinate value lies in demonstrating the great practice of cultural consciousness, flesh-and-blood interfacing, and saving the nation through science.

### Chapter 1: Quantum Collisions and Paradigm Overhauls in the Microscopic World

#### Section 1: Photoelectric Entanglement and Quantum Scattering—Einstein's Light Quantum Hypothesis VS Wu Youxun's Decisive Verification on Fifteen Elements

##### 【Western Background: Proposal of Light Quantum Hypothesis and the Compton Scattering Dispute】
In 1905, Albert Einstein published his paper on the light quantum, extending Planck's concept of energy quanta to light itself, proposing the "Light Quantum" (Photon) hypothesis, and successfully explaining the photoelectric effect. However, this hypothesis, which violated the classical intuition of Maxwell's wave equations, encountered strong skepticism in academic circles for more than a decade. Even Planck and Millikan initially held reservations about the physical reality of light quanta.
In 1923, Arthur H. Compton published his theory of X-ray scattering, pointing out that X-rays experience a wavelength shift when colliding with electrons, and treated this process as a relativistic elastic collision between a photon and a free electron. This theory triggered fierce refutations from classical electromagnetic wave theorists. William Duane of Harvard University and other scholars claimed through experiments that they failed to observe this shift, arguing that it was merely a secondary effect of the scattering target material. The quantum photon theory and the wave theory engaged in a fierce paradigm clash in the field of microscopic scattering.

##### 【Chinese Counterpoint: Wu Youxun's Systematic Verification of Fifteen Elements】
At the critical moment of the Compton theory's survival, Wu Youxun, a Chinese student at the University of Chicago, carried out decisive experimental work. With extremely superb experimental skills and rigorous data, Wu Youxun systematically measured the wavelength shifts of X-rays scattered by different elements.
The core of the debate in the academic community at that time was whether the wavelength shift depended on the atomic number $Z$ of the scattering substance. Classical wave theory predicted that it should depend on the internal electron distribution and nuclear structure of the target material. Wu Youxun used 15 different chemical elements, both pure elements and compounds, ranging from lithium ( $Z=3$ ) to lead ( $Z=82$ ) as target materials, to precisely measure their X-ray scattering spectra at the same scattering angle.
The experimental data clearly demonstrated that, at the same scattering angle, the wavelength shift $\Delta \lambda$ remained constant and strictly independent of the physical properties of the scattering medium. Wu Youxun also tackled the scattering of heavy elements (such as lead), eliminating the interference caused by tightly bound electrons, confirming that Compton scattering remains valid even in heavy elements. This series of perfect experiments by Wu Youxun directly silenced the doubts of Duane and others, becoming the most comprehensive and indisputable crucial experimental evidence for the Compton effect and the light quantum hypothesis.

> [!TIP]
> **Physical Mechanism of Compton Scattering Wavelength Shift and Target Independence**
> Treat the collision between a high-frequency X-ray and an electron in the target as a relativistic energy and momentum conservation process.
> Let the wavelength of the incident photon be $\lambda$ , the wavelength of the scattered photon be $\lambda'$ , and the scattering angle be $\theta$ . From the relativistic conservation laws:
> $$ p_{\gamma} + p_e = p_{\gamma}' + p_e' $$
> $$ E_{\gamma} + m_e c^2 = E_{\gamma}' + E_e' $$
> The wavelength shift formula can be precisely derived as:
> $$ \Delta \lambda = \lambda' - \lambda = \frac{h}{m_e c} (1 - \cos \theta) $$
> where $\lambda_C = \frac{h}{m_e c} \approx 2.426 \times 10^{-12} \text{ m}$ is called the Compton wavelength of the electron.
>
> **Decisive Significance of Wu Youxun's Experiment**:
> Classical wave theory predicted that the wavelength change should depend on the extra-nuclear electron structure of the scattering element (i.e., depending on $Z$ ). Wu Youxun used 15 elements from lithium ( $Z=3$ ) to lead ( $Z=82$ ) to perform precise measurements, proving that at the same scattering angle, the wavelength shift $\Delta \lambda$ remains constant, strictly independent of the physical properties of the scattering medium. This provided irrefutable proof that the scattering is a two-body relativistic collision between a photon and a "free electron," offering the most critical experimental support for the establishment of the light quantum theory.

#### Section 2: The Dirac Sea and the Dawn of Antimatter—The Relativistic Wave Equation VS Zhao Zhongyao's Anomalous Absorption and Pair Annihilation

##### 【Western Background: Prediction of Relativistic Quantum Mechanics and the Fog of Searching for the Positron】
In 1928, the British physicist Paul Dirac proposed the wave equation describing relativistic electrons—the Dirac equation. This equation not only perfectly integrated quantum mechanics and special relativity but also naturally derived the spin and magnetic moment of the electron. However, the equation also produced a puzzling negative energy solution.
To resolve the physical crisis of "atoms collapsing to infinitely low energy" caused by the negative energy solution, Dirac proposed the famous "hole theory" (Dirac Sea) in 1930: the vacuum is filled with electrons of all negative energy states. If one of the negative energy electrons is excited to a positive energy state, it leaves a "hole." This hole, carrying a positive charge and having the same mass as the electron, is the "anti-electron" (positron).
However, at the beginning of this theory's proposal, the experimental physics community was in a state of confusion. Most people regarded negative energy states as a mathematical fiction, and even Einstein and Bohr were skeptical. At that time, Carl Anderson was using a cloud chamber to observe cosmic rays but had not yet confirmed the track of the positron.

##### 【Chinese Counterpoint: Zhao Zhongyao's First Observation of Electron-Positron Pair Production and Double-Photon Annihilation】
On the eve of this paradigm dawn, Zhao Zhongyao, a Chinese student in the laboratory of Robert Millikan at the California Institute of Technology, completed two great experiments.
In 1930, Zhao Zhongyao used the ThC" source to generate high-energy $\gamma$ rays of $2.62 \text{ MeV}$ to irradiate heavy targets such as lead, measuring their absorption coefficients. He discovered that the actual absorption coefficient of $\gamma$ rays in lead was significantly larger than the electronic scattering value predicted by the Klein-Nishina formula. This excess absorption (anomalous absorption) was physically the process in which high-energy $\gamma$ photons transformed into an electron-positron pair in the strong electric field of the nucleus, namely **pair production (Pair Production)**, which is significantly above the starting threshold of $2 m_e c^2 \approx 1.02 \text{ MeV}$ .
Immediately after, Zhao Zhongyao performed an energy analysis on the scattered radiation. He observed an isotropic, special secondary radiation with an energy of approximately $0.5 \text{ MeV}$ , whose wavelength did not vary with the scattering angle. At that time, no one could explain the source of this special radiation. This was physically the first experimental proof of **electron-positron pair annihilation (Pair Annihilation)**, where positrons decelerated in the medium and met negative electrons in the environment, releasing two isotropic photons each with an energy of $0.511 \text{ MeV}$ .
Zhao Zhongyao's experiment was the first time in human history that the complete physical process of antimatter production and annihilation was observed. Anderson later admitted in his memoirs that it was Zhao Zhongyao's experimental results that suggested to him that this anomalous absorption might originate from some new particle, thereby guiding him to capture the classic track of the positron in the cloud chamber.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Quantitative Calculation of Positron Pair Production Threshold and Annihilation Radiation Energy**
> 1. **Pair Production Threshold**:
>    A high-energy $\gamma$ photon transforms into a positron and an electron in the strong magnetic/electric field of an atomic nucleus. Due to conservation of energy, the minimum energy of the photon must equal the sum of the rest masses of the generated positron and electron:
>    $$ E_{\text{threshold}} \ge 2 m_e c^2 \approx 2 \times 0.511 \text{ MeV} = 1.022 \text{ MeV} $$
>    The ThC" photon energy of $2.62 \text{ MeV}$ used in Zhao Zhongyao's experiment was significantly higher than $1.022 \text{ MeV}$ , thus triggering pair production efficiently. This was the fundamental physical reason for his observation of "excess absorption" (anomalous absorption) in lead.
> 
> 2. **Pair Annihilation Energy**:
>    When the generated positron slows down to a thermal kinetic state in the medium, it annihilates with an electron in the environment:
>    $$ e^+ + e^- \to 2\gamma $$
>    By conservation of momentum, the two annihilation photons must be emitted in opposite directions (at an angle of $180^\circ$ ); by conservation of energy, the energy of each photon is:
>    $$ E_{\gamma} = m_e c^2 \approx 0.511 \text{ MeV} $$
>    In 1930, Zhao Zhongyao measured an isotropic "special radiation" whose wavelength did not drift with the scattering angle, with an energy of about $0.5 \text{ MeV}$ . This was the first experimental observation in human history of the $0.511 \text{ MeV}$ double photons released by positron-electron annihilation.

#### Section 3: Weak Interactions and the Overthrow of Parity Symmetry—The Theoretical Revolution of Tsung-Dao Lee and Yang Chen-Ning VS Wu Chien-shiung's Low-Temperature Cobalt-60 Experiment

##### 【Western Background: The $\theta-\tau$ Puzzle and the Shaking of the Creed of Parity Conservation】
In the mid-1950s, the particle physics community encountered the famous " $\theta-\tau$ decay puzzle": two types of mesons with identical masses and lifetimes decayed into two pions (even parity) and three pions (odd parity), respectively. If parity (space reflection symmetry) is conserved in all physical processes, they had to be two different particles, which severely contradicted their identical masses and lifetimes.
In 1956, the Chinese theoretical physicists Tsung-Dao Lee and Yang Chen-Ning collaborated deeply at the Princeton Institute for Advanced Study and published an epoch-making paper. They systematically reviewed all known experiments and pointed out that, although parity conservation was well established in strong and electromagnetic interactions, it had never been experimentally tested in weak interactions. They boldly proposed that parity is not conserved in weak decays and conceived a series of polarized nuclear decay experiment schemes to test this effect.

##### 【Chinese Counterpoint: Wu Chien-shiung and the Peak Confrontation of Low-Temperature Nuclear Polarization Experiment】
Facing this hypothesis that shattered a classic physical belief, the vast majority of Western experimental physicists (including masters like Pauli and Feynman) held extreme skepticism, and Pauli even proposed to bet heavily that "God is not a weak left-hander."
Only the experimental master Wu Chien-shiung keenly perceived the revolutionary significance of this work. She immediately cancelled her vacation plans and collaborated with low-temperature physicists at the National Bureau of Standards (NBS) to design the exquisite polarized Cobalt-60 ( $^{60}\text{Co}$ ) $\beta$ decay experiment.
The physical difficulty of the experiment was extremely high: the Cobalt-60 nuclei had to be highly polarized using a strong magnetic field at an ultra-low temperature of $0.01 \text{ K}$ to overcome the thermal motion of the atoms. At the same time, a scintillation detector capable of detecting $\beta$ electrons had to be installed inside the ultra-low temperature vacuum chamber.
By the end of 1956, Wu Chien-shiung's team obtained solid data: as the spin polarization of the nuclei was established, the emission probability of $\beta$ particles on the side opposite to the polarization direction was significantly higher than on the same side. The electron emission exhibited a striking spatial asymmetry. This proved that space reflection symmetry is completely broken in weak interactions, representing maximum parity violation. This experimental result directly led to the 1957 Nobel Prize in Physics for Tsung-Dao Lee and Yang Chen-Ning, and also established Wu Chien-shiung's status as one of the world's most outstanding experimental physics masters.

> [!NOTE]
> **Asymmetric Physical Characterization of Nuclear Polarization and Electron Emission Angular Distribution in Weak Decays**
> To measure whether parity is conserved in weak interactions, one must construct a pseudoscalar operator that changes sign under space reflection, and measure its expectation value.
> The most direct pseudoscalar is the dot product of the electron momentum $\mathbf{p}_e$ and the nuclear spin $\mathbf{J}$ : $\langle \mathbf{J} \cdot \mathbf{p}_e \rangle$ .
> Under space reflection: $\mathbf{p}_e \to -\mathbf{p}_e$ (polar vector), $\mathbf{J} \to \mathbf{J}$ (axial vector), so the dot product changes sign. If parity is conserved, the statistical expectation value of this dot product must be zero.
>
> Using polarized Cobalt-60 ( $^{60}\text{Co}$ ) $\beta$ decay at ultra-low temperatures, the angular distribution function of the emitted electrons is expressed as:
> $$ W(\theta) = 1 + A \frac{\langle J_z \rangle}{J} \frac{v}{c} \cos\theta $$
> where $\theta$ is the angle between the electron emission direction and the nuclear spin direction (the direction of the polarizing external magnetic field), $\langle J_z \rangle/J$ is the polarization degree, $v/c$ is the relativistic velocity of the electron, and $A$ is the parity-non-conserving asymmetry parameter.
>
> **Wu Chien-shiung's Experimental Determination**:
> Wu Chien-shiung's experiment measured the asymmetry parameter $A \approx -1$ (representing maximum parity violation). This meant that electrons tended to be emitted at angles opposite to the nuclear spin polarization ( $\theta \approx 180^\circ$ ). This result directly overthrew the creed of classical space reflection symmetry.

### Chapter 2: The Flesh-and-Blood Institutionalization of Chinese Physics

#### Section 1: Transoceanic Transplantation of Discipline Paradigms—The Euro-American Scientific Community and the Academic Pedigree of the "Four Great Mentors"

##### 【Western Background: Rise of Euro-American Modern Physics Research Communities】
In the first half of the 20th century, Western modern physics completed the transition from workshop-style individual observations to state-level scientific communities and university research laboratories. Göttingen and Munich in Germany, Copenhagen in Denmark, and Harvard, Princeton, and the University of Chicago in the US established highly institutionalized graduate training and laboratory systems. In these systems, theoretical breakthroughs and precise experimental techniques complemented each other, forming academic pedigrees passed down through generations.

##### 【Chinese Counterpoint: The Scientific Foundation Built from Scratch by the Four Great Mentors】
In China, which lacked any modern physics foundation, the first generation of physicists who studied in Europe and America and received the most advanced quantum and experimental training initiated a miracle of discipline institutionalization. Hailed as the "Four Great Mentors," Hu Gangfu, Rao Yutai, Ye Qisun, and Wu Youxun, with resilient responsibility, transplanted Western laboratory training and rigorous research paradigms to China:
1. **Hu Gangfu's Experimental Pioneering**: Harvard PhD, studying under high-pressure physics master Bridgman. After returning, he pioneered physics laboratories at Nanjing Higher Normal School and Zhejiang University, making instruments by hand, introducing advanced X-ray spectroscopy research, and training pioneers like Wu Youxun and Yan Jici through high-intensity laboratory practice.
2. **Rao Yutai's Spectroscopy and Talent Cultivation**: Princeton PhD, studying under Nobel laureate Richardson. He founded the Physics Department of Nankai University and chaired the Physics Department of Peking University for a long time, dedicated to spectroscopy and molecular structure research. His rigorous style deeply shaped the Peking University school of physics.
3. **Ye Qisun's Scientific Management and Grand Design**: Harvard PhD, who not only performed outstanding work in precisely determining the Planck constant $h$ (measuring $h = 6.556 \times 10^{-27} \text{ erg sec}$ in the 1921 Duane-Palmer-Yeh experiment) but was also the founder of the Tsinghua Physics Department and School of Science. He implemented the policies of "professors governing the university" and "quality over quantity," building Tsinghua Physics Department into the academic stronghold of China.
4. **Wu Youxun's Academic Institution and Rigorous Style**: Returned to China after verifying the Compton effect at the University of Chicago, chairing physics departments at Tsinghua and Central University. He fully transplanted the experimental management paradigm of the Chicago school, laying the technical foundation for China's native nuclear physics and cosmic ray experiments.

Starting from scratch, they established the Chinese Physical Society and the Institute of Physics of Academia Sinica, building a solid academic chain of "First-generation Mentors $\to$ Second-generation Backbone (Founding Fathers of Two Bombs, One Satellite) $\to$ Third-generation Scholars."

#### Section 2: Sparks of Reason in the Flames of War—Frontier Lectures and the Miracle of Southwest Associated University's Thatched Huts

##### 【Western Background: The Nuclear Wild Run of Western Physics on the Eve of World War II】
On the eve of World War II, Western physics was marching rapidly from quantum mechanics to nuclear physics and quantum field theory. Solvay Conferences continuously discussed nuclear fission and neutron-induced radioactivity, and Western university laboratories rapidly turned to wartime defense physics and atomic energy development under state support.

##### 【Chinese Counterpoint: The Academic Miracle Under Southwest Associated University's Thatched Huts】
In 1937, the Anti-Japanese War broke out comprehensively, and Tsinghua, Peking University, and Nankai migrated to Kunming to establish Southwest Associated University. Under the extreme difficulties of dodging air raids, extreme shortage of materials, and classrooms with thatched roofs and dirt floors, the Physics Department maintained teaching and research levels comparable to world-class universities.
1. **World Frontier in Thatched Huts**: Under dim candlelight and sirens, Zhou Peiyuan taught general relativity, Wu Ta-You taught quantum mechanics and atomic spectra, and Wang Zhuxi taught thermodynamics and statistical physics, directly tracking the latest global literature.
2. **Self-Reliant Experimental Adherence**: When instruments were destroyed, physicists used scrap metal and old car parts to modify them into experimental devices, persisting in basic physics experiment courses to ensure that the experimental paradigm was not interrupted by war.
3. **Igniting the Spark of the Future**: In the thatched huts of Southwest Associated University, Yang Chen-Ning and Tsung-Dao Lee laid the most solid foundation of physics. Wu Ta-You discovered Tsung-Dao Lee's talent and recommended him to study in the US. The guidance of Wang Zhuxi and Wu Ta-You profoundly influenced Yang Chen-Ning's academic direction in statistical mechanics and group theory symmetry. Besides Yang and Lee, the university also trained Deng Jiaxian, Zhu Guangya, Zhao Jiuzhang, and other "Two Bombs, One Satellite" founding fathers, completing a scientific miracle in the history of world education and science with the vitality of "flesh-and-blood interfacing" in the war.

## Conclusion and Revelations: Modern Reflection on Scientific Thinking

The half-century from 1900 to 1949 was a period when Chinese physicists used their passion and flesh to directly "interface" the modern physical science edifice, which took hundreds of years to develop in the West, onto the ruins of China. From the return of the four great mentors to the continuous teaching under the thatched huts of Southwest Associated University, and then to the outstanding contributions of Wu Chien-shiung, Zhao Zhongyao, and Wu Youxun at the highest peaks of world physics, this history provides us with profound revelations on modern science education and basic research:

1. **The Belief Drive of "Flesh-and-Blood Interfacing"**:
   Hu Gangfu, Ye Qisun, and others gave up superior research conditions in the West to return to teach in China, sacrificing their own creative potential during their golden academic periods to dedicate their entire lives to building laboratories and education systems from scratch. This reveals that the development and institutionalization of basic science, in the early stage, requires not only funds and equipment but also outstanding educators who act as stepping stones with cultural consciousness and a spirit of responsibility.

2. **Adhering to "Experiment as the Decisive Standard"**:
   Whether it was Wu Youxun using 15 elements to thoroughly solidify the light quantum hypothesis, Zhao Zhongyao persisting in his experimental data to verify annihilation radiation under bias and skepticism, or Wu Chien-shiung precisely detecting Cobalt-60 at ultra-low temperatures under the doubt of Pauli and others through extreme craftsmanship, the first generation of Chinese physicists demonstrated an peerless scientific spirit of "valuing proof, not blindly following." Modern education should inherit this style of treating experiment as the highest judge of physics, correcting the old habits of emphasizing test theory, neglecting practical hands-on work, and blindly worshiping authority.

3. **Melting Elite Mentorship and Independent Thinking**:
   The miracle of Southwest Associated University demonstrates that a first-class academic community and educational effect do not depend solely on buildings and equipment, but lie in the "close mentorship inheritance between masters and disciples" and "the pure desire for knowledge stimulated in extreme environments." In the thatched huts dodging bombing, the equal exchange between teachers and students around the latest quantum mechanics literature nurtured Yang Chen-Ning and Tsung-Dao Lee's independent thinking ability to dare to question and revolutionize "physical dogmas" like parity conservation. This provides an important reference for the reform of modern higher education: we must shorten the spatial and intellectual distance between teachers and students, giving students independent space for autonomous thinking and questioning the frontier.
