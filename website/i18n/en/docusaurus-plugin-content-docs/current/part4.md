# Part 4: Defense Physics and Institutional Cohesion under Cold War Geopolitics (1949–1978)

> *"To establish a heart for heaven and earth, to establish a life for the living people, to continue the absolute learning for the past sages, to open peace for all generations."* —— Zhang Zai

![Defense Physics in Desert with Dragon and Camel Caravan Illustration](pathname:///img/part4_illustration.png)

## World Axis
After World War II, the world entered the Cold War era of bipolar confrontation between the United States and the Soviet Union. The development model of physics was fundamentally reshaped, moving from "Small Science" to "Big Science." Nuclear physics, particle physics, semiconductor physics, and aerospace technology became the core of major power competition. The success of the Manhattan Project established the status of post-war physics as the "first discipline," and physics was deeply bound with national security, geopolitics, and industrial capabilities. The scale of scientific discovery was unprecedentedly increased, but its independence was also reshaped to a large extent by the national will. Physicists moved from the ivory tower to the front line of national strategy, becoming a key force in determining the contrast of major power forces.

## China Point
In 1949, the People's Republic of China was established, facing a severe international environment and nuclear threats. In the cracks of the Cold War, China made the strategic decision to independently develop defense physics centered on "Two Bombs, One Satellite" (atomic bomb, hydrogen bomb, artificial satellite). A large number of overseas wanderers (such as Qian Xuesen, Qian Sanqiang, Peng Huanwu, Deng Jiaxian, Guo Yonghuai, etc.) resolutely returned to China. China established a highly centralized "Whole-Nation System" playing as a game of chess. Under extreme difficulties with lack of materials, simple equipment, and the breakdown of Sino-Soviet relations, physicists in the depths of the desert used abacuses and hand-cranked computers to miraculously complete the research and development of nuclear weapons and satellites, laying the security foundation of new China.

## Coordinate Value
The coordinate value of this period was reflected in the deep integration of Chinese physics with the national destiny and the unique characteristics of "institutional cohesion." Under Cold War geopolitical pressure, Chinese physics took a unique road of "tasks leading disciplines." Although the broad exploration of basic theories was restricted, astonishing leaps were achieved in the field of defense cutting-edge technology. **Faced with the famous "Needham's Question"—that is, why modern science was not born in China, the physicists of new China gave a practical answer regarding "how a late-comer country achieves scientific transition" under extreme difficulties using the whole-nation system.** The "Two Bombs, One Satellite" spirit demonstrated by Chinese physicists was a perfect combination of scientific spirit and patriotism, completely changing the coordinate position of China in the world pattern and demonstrating the possibility for a developing country to achieve scientific and technological self-reliance and self-improvement under extreme conditions.

## Content

### Chapter 1: The Era of Big Science and the Clouds of the Cold War

#### I. The Manhattan Project and the Birth of "Big Science"
The Manhattan Project not only changed the form of war but also reshaped the scientific research model, marking the arrival of the "Big Science" era. Previously, physics research was mainly based on individuals or small laboratories, driven by curiosity. The Manhattan Project mobilized hundreds of thousands of personnel, costing over 2 billion US dollars, and concentrated the top physicists in the West, such as Oppenheimer, Fermi, Feynman, etc. This model driven by national will, supported by huge funds, and featuring large-scale interdisciplinary collaboration became the paradigm of post-war scientific research. **From the perspective of Thomas Kuhn's philosophy of science, this was not only an expansion of the scale of scientific research but also a "Paradigm Shift" in scientific research models.** Physics henceforth walked out of the ivory tower and became a strategic resource deciding the survival of the nation. The institutionalization and scale of science reached an unprecedented height, marking that science entered the era of national monopoly.

#### II. Weaponization of Physics under the Cold War Pattern
During the Cold War, physics inevitably became the most eye-catching "weaponized" discipline. Nuclear monopoly and anti-monopoly were the focus of the US-Soviet game. The development of nuclear physics was endowed with the supreme property of national secret. The status of physicists became complex; they were both national heroes and subject to strict political review. **Oppenheimer, as the hero of the Manhattan Project, was politically persecuted by McCarthyism after the war due to his reserved attitude toward the development of the hydrogen bomb, falling into a deep moral predicament and political crisis. In sharp contrast, on the other side of the ocean, Chinese physicists such as Deng Jiaxian, despite facing extremely simple material conditions, achieved unprecedented spiritual cohesion and unity of will under the noble goal of serving the country. This intersection of physicists' destinies under different systems constituted the most thought-provoking scene in the history of Cold War science.** Oppenheimer's encounter reflected the strong regulation and co-optation of scientific will by political power, while the choice of Chinese physicists demonstrated another responsibility of intellectuals under national crisis.

Besides nuclear weapons, the Cold War also greatly stimulated the development of other branches of physics. The continuous improvement of radar technology promoted microwave physics and quantum electronics, ultimately giving birth to the invention of the laser; to meet the needs of miniaturization and high reliability of military electronic equipment, semiconductor physics grew explosively under the strong injection of military funds, making solid-state physics (predecessor of condensed matter physics) the most massive branch in physics. Scientific competition essentially became a battlefield of geopolitical extension, and the development track of physics was deeply branded with the mark of the Cold War.

#### III. Sputnik Moment and Space Race
In 1957, the Soviet Union launched the first artificial satellite "Sputnik 1," triggering panic in the West, known in history as the "Sputnik Moment." This completely activated the scientific potential of the United States, leading to the establishment of NASA and the Apollo moon landing plan. The space race greatly promoted physics research related to aerospace. Physicists needed to solve the "blackout" problem generated by high-temperature gas ionization when spacecraft re-entered the atmosphere, which promoted the development of plasma physics. Meanwhile, hypersonic aerodynamics, space astronomy, and cosmic ray physics also advanced wildly in this context, and space became the new frontier of big power competition.

#### IV. Scientific Exchange and Institutional Competition under the Iron Curtain
Despite strict blockades, the universality of physics still gave birth to unique exchanges. The European Organization for Nuclear Research (CERN), established in 1954, was a product of European countries jointly countering the US and Soviet Union to avoid brain drain. Its principle of open results made it a unique platform for exchange between the East and the West. The Soviet Union established the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Dubna as a response. China actively participated in the work of Dubna in the 1950s, and Wang Ganchang and others made major discoveries such as the anti-sigma minus hyperon there, training a batch of high-energy physics talents for China until the breakdown of Sino-Soviet relations. This state of both confrontation and exchange constituted a unique landscape of Cold War science.

### Chapter 2: The Whole-Nation System and the Miracle of "Two Bombs, One Satellite"

#### I. Historical Choice: New China Facing Nuclear Threats
When new China was established in 1949, the industrial base was almost zero, and there were only hundreds of professional physics researchers in the country. In terms of geopolitics, new China was in a Western blockade as soon as it was established. After the outbreak of the Korean War, the United States publicly or secretly threatened to use atomic bombs against China many times. Faced with realistic and urgent nuclear blackmail, in January 1955, the CPC Central Committee made the strategic decision to develop the atomic energy cause. Mao Zedong pointed out: "If we don't want to be bullied, we cannot do without this thing." This decision directly raised the development of physics to the highest national strategic interest, opening the most magnificent chapter in the history of Chinese physics.

#### II. The Tide of Science Returning and Elite Cohesion
To develop defense physics, talent was the key. In the 1950s, a large number of overseas Chinese scientists broke through resistance to return to China. Qian Xuesen overcame five years of house arrest by the US to return, establishing the foundation of China's aerospace aerodynamics, pushing China's missile and aerospace undertakings forward by at least twenty years; Qian Sanqiang, as a nuclear physics expert, became an outstanding organizer and "general designer" of the atomic energy cause; Peng Huanwu presided over the theoretical design of nuclear weapons and solved a large number of basic physical problems; Deng Jiaxian, as the director of the theoretical department, personally participated in and led the theoretical design of the atomic bomb and hydrogen bomb; Guo Yonghuai made key contributions to mechanical design and used his body to protect extremely precious test data when the plane crashed. The gathering of this batch of elites enabled China to have a world-class group of physicists in a short time, and they completely integrated their personal academic pursuits into the national destiny.

#### III. The Whole-Nation System "Playing as a Game of Chess"
China was able to create a miracle because of the "Whole-Nation System" at its core. In 1962, a central special committee chaired by Zhou Enlai was established, implementing unified leadership with absolute authority. This system maximized the overcoming of resource dispersion and was the guarantee for China to achieve technological leap under extremely poor conditions. Under this system, Chinese physics demonstrated characteristics of "institutional cohesion": first, "tasks leading disciplines," atomic nuclear physics, neutron physics, fluid mechanics, high-temperature and high-pressure physics were all strongly pulled by tasks; second, cross-departmental large collaboration involving hundreds of institutes and factories such as the Chinese Academy of Sciences, the Second Ministry of Machine Building, and the Seventh Ministry of Machine Building; third, a dedicated culture of anonymity, where scientists remained anonymous in the desert, overcoming harsh natural environments and scarce material conditions, willing to be nameless heroes.

#### IV. From Nine Calculations to the First Atomic Bomb
In 1959, the Soviet Union unilaterally tore up the contract and withdrew experts, and China's nuclear cause faced a fundamental crisis. The CPC Central Committee decided to "do it ourselves, start from scratch," naming the first atomic bomb "596." **That was a strange confrontation between the most cutting-edge modern science and the most primitive tools. In the lack of large computers, Deng Jiaxian, Zhou Guangzao, and others used abacuses, slide rules, and hand-cranked computers to perform massive calculations. In the desert night, there were only the clicking sounds of abacus beads and the roaring sounds of hand-cranked computers, forming a shocking contrast with the violent nuclear reaction in one hundred billionth of a second inside the atomic bomb.** The implosion design of the atomic bomb required extremely high precision of detonation wave focusing, involving extremely complex fluid mechanics and neutron transport equations. In the famous "Nine Calculations," regarding a key physical parameter, the calculation results of the Chinese side did not match the data left by the Soviet experts. **This was not only a divergence of data but also a silent contest between Chinese native physical wisdom and Soviet scientific authority.** Zhou Guangzao, with his profound theoretical foundation, used the principle of maximum thermodynamics to strictly prove the correctness of the Chinese side's calculation from theory, pointing out the physical impossibility of the Soviet side's data, clearing the barrier for theoretical design. On October 16, 1964, China's first atomic bomb exploded successfully in Lop Nur.

#### V. Yu Min Configuration and China Speed
From the atomic bomb to the hydrogen bomb, the United States took more than 7 years, while China took only 2 years and 8 months, creating "China Speed." **To understand this speed more intuitively, we can compare the time spent by various countries from the first atomic bomb to the first hydrogen bomb: the United States 7 years and 3 months, the Soviet Union 4 years, the United Kingdom 4 years and 7 months, France 8 years and 6 months, while China took only 2 years and 8 months.** This was due to the outstanding contribution of the native physicist Yu Min. Yu Min had never studied abroad, but he had extremely high attainments in nuclear theory. The principle of the hydrogen bomb was far more complex than the atomic bomb; it was not simple fission but required using fission energy to ignite fusion materials, involving radiation fluid mechanics under extremely high temperature and high pressure. In the "Hundred-Day Campaign" in 1965, Yu Min, with his keen physical intuition, tackled the key physical mechanism of radiation implosion and proposed a unique theoretical scheme for the hydrogen bomb, known as the "Yu Min Configuration," ranking with the Teller-Ulam configuration in the US as the only two successful hydrogen bomb configurations in the world. On June 17, 1967, China's first hydrogen bomb exploded successfully, completing a shocking leap.

#### VI. Dongfanghong-1 and Aerospace Physics Foundation
While developing the "Two Bombs," China also entered space. Under the leadership of Zhao Jiuzhang, Qian Xuesen, and others, physicists tackled aerospace physics problems such as satellite temperature control, attitude control, and space environment adaptation. They designed an exquisite radiation heat transfer scheme, solving the problem of instruments working under extreme temperature differences in space, and ensured stable transmission of radio signals in harsh environments. On April 24, 1970, China's first satellite "Dongfanghong-1" was launched successfully, marking that China stepped into the ranks of major space powers and completed the great cause of "Two Bombs, One Satellite."

### 【Scientific Thinking and Education Revelations】
The development of Chinese physics in this period, although under the special background of the Cold War and geopolitics, still has profound revelations for today's science education shown in the scientific thinking demonstrated therein:
1. **Physical Intuition is More Important than Complex Calculations**: Yu Min, in the research and development of the hydrogen bomb, with his keen physical intuition, grasped the key mechanism of "radiation implosion" in massive data. This reveals to us that in education, attention should be paid to cultivating students' intuitive understanding of physical images rather than pure mathematical derivation.
2. **Critical Thinking Based on First Principles**: Zhou Guangzao, in the "Nine Calculations," dared to question the data of Soviet experts and strictly proved it with the principle of maximum thermodynamics. This ability to not blindly follow authority and start from basic physical principles to conduct independent thinking is the core of science education.
3. **Target-Driven and Cross-Disciplinary Collaboration**: The model of "tasks leading disciplines" proved that clear goals and cross-field collaboration can greatly stimulate innovation potential. In modern education, project-based learning (PBL) and interdisciplinary education can learn from this model.

### Conclusion: Institutional Cohesion and "Geopolitical Reshaping" of Disciplines
Between 1949 and 1978, Chinese physics, under Cold War pressure, realized a high degree of "institutional cohesion" through the "Whole-Nation System." This model, though suppressing the broad exploration of basic theories to a certain extent and making some pure theoretical disciplines develop leanly, successfully concentrated limited resources in defense cutting-edge fields, established a complete nuclear industry and aerospace system, and trained a generation of compound physics talents who understood both theory and engineering. The success of "Two Bombs, One Satellite" reshaped the coordinates of China in the world strategic pattern, and science here became the highest wisdom crystallization of an ancient civilization seeking independence and security.

This period of institutional cohesion, although a product of specific historical conditions, the model of "large collaboration" and "tackling difficulties" it established still has important revelatory significance for scientific and technological breakthroughs in key technology fields in China today. It tells us that when facing external geopolitical pressure, concentrating forces and relying on ourselves are effective ways to break blockades. The struggle of Chinese physics in this period not only laid an indispensable material and spiritual foundation for later reform and opening up and scientific and technological take-off but also wrote a unique and magnificent chapter in the history of world science.
